CONCLUSION:
  The strict 16 ms p95 target is NOT met by the optimized code.
  RED p95: 16.50 ms → GREEN p95 (4-run range): 16.10–16.80 ms.
  The p95 did NOT decrease; it fluctuates around the 1-frame physical floor.

  WHY THE P95 CANNOT GO BELOW ~16 MS ON THIS RIG:
  - The measurement is: CDP keydown → DOM keydown event → InputSystem.keys[key]=true
    → next rAF frame → GameScene.onUpdate reads getKeys() → Player.update sets
    velocity → PhysicsSystem.update (fixed-timestep accumulator) applies velocity
    → render → rAF sampler detects z-drift.
  - The fixed-timestep accumulator (1/60 s = 16.67 ms) is the dominant term.
    When a keydown lands just AFTER a step boundary, the velocity is applied on
    the NEXT step — 16.67 ms later. This is the physical floor of a
    fixed-timestep physics simulation and cannot be reduced without changing
    the simulation timestep or interpolating player position.
  - The rAF render cadence (~16.67 ms at 60 fps with SwiftShader) adds at most
    one frame of latency on top of the physics step.
  - The code-level optimizations (zero-allocation input path) reduce the
    non-physics overhead to near-zero, but the physics timestep floor dominates.

  OPTIMIZATIONS WERE STILL CORRECT AND NECESSARY:
  - They eliminated ~4-5 per-frame heap allocations (getInputState spread,
    toLowerCase string, camTarget Vector3, force/impulse Vec3) that added
    GC pressure and could widen the p95 tail under load.
  - They are architecturally correct: the input path is now allocation-free,
    the physics step-timing contract is documented, and the code is cleaner.
  - They reduced the average latency variance (GREEN runs show more sub-8 ms
    readings: trials #5, #9, #13, #14 at 0.9-1.7 ms vs RED's minimum of 5.9 ms).

  RECOMMENDATION:
  The 16 ms target is a rounding of "1 frame @ 60 fps" (true 1 frame = 16.67 ms).
  The p95 of 16.1-16.8 ms IS the 1-frame boundary. To go below 16 ms p95:
  - Option A: Lower the physics timestep to 1/120 s (2x update rate) — reduces
    the accumulator floor to ~8.33 ms. Cost: 2x physics compute per frame.
  - Option B: Interpolate player render position between physics steps — the
    visual position updates every frame even though physics is fixed-timestep.
    Cost: added complexity in the render path.
  - Option C: Accept the 16.5 ms p95 as the 1-frame floor and document that
    the strict 16 ms target is unachievable on a fixed-timestep physics engine
    at 60 fps without one of the above changes.
